# Solution for Lesson 25: Automation consulting: the audit as the entry product

Running the scorer on the shipped dental intake gives an overall score of 39. The dimensions, weakest first, are: follow-up discipline at 10 (the practice never follows up an enquiry that goes quiet), booking ease at 30 (phone only, during opening hours), after-hours coverage at 34 (55 percent of enquiries arrive when nobody is on duty), response speed at 35 (first replies take hours), capacity headroom at 50, and channel coverage at 75.

The worked recommendation follows the ordering. Fix by hand first: a same-day callback list for quiet enquiries, owned by the front desk, costs nothing and tests whether follow-up even converts for this practice. Automate second: an after-hours first response with qualification questions and a booking link, scoped and priced as its own engagement. The honest do-nothing case is also written down: channel coverage is not the problem, so no new channel tooling is recommended at all, and if the callback list shows quiet enquiries rarely convert, the automation recommendation shrinks with it. That is the audit earning its fee: the client paid for the order of the work, not for a pitch.
