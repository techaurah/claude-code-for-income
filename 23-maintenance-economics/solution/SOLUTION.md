# Solution for Lesson 23: Maintenance economics: keeping a product alive cheaply

The fixture ledger is the worked solution, and its arithmetic is worth doing once by hand. Tickets: 120 users at 8 per 100 gives 9.6 tickets a month. Support: 9.6 tickets at 18 minutes is 172.8 minutes, just under three hours, which at $40 an hour is $115.20. Agent maintenance: $1.60 a run at the October 2026 Sonnet class list prices, times 12 runs, is $19.20. Fixed costs are therefore $64.20, hosting plus agent runs. Support cost per user is $0.96, so contribution per user is $15 minus $0.96, which is $14.04, and break-even is $64.20 divided by $14.04, rounded up: 5 users.

Read what the solution is really showing. At this scale the product is cheap to keep alive and the margin per user is wide, because the support load is light, the Lesson 21 maintenance criterion doing its work two lessons later. A heavier product, at 30 tickets per 100 users and 40 minutes a ticket, carries a support cost per user of $13.33 at the same rate, a contribution of $1.67, and a break-even near forty users before agent costs grow. Same price, same builder, different tail. That contrast, not the fixture's tidy numbers, is the lesson.
